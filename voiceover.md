# Fiado voiceover

Mustapha Fadhlullah, independent security researcher

This is the script to read over the video in `demo.md`. About 320 words, about 2 minutes 10 seconds at an easy speaking pace. Each part is timed to the scene with the same number.

## How to record it

- Record in a quiet room, close to the microphone. Read it aloud twice before you record.
- Speak slower than feels natural. Judges are reading captions and watching the screen at the same time.
- Leave a short pause after the refusal in scene 2. Let the red stamp land before you speak again.
- Say "naira" for ₦. Say "testnet" the way you would say "test network".
- Record each scene as its own file. It is easier to redo one scene than the whole take.
- If a line feels stiff when you say it, change it to how you would say it to a shopkeeper. The meaning has to stay the same.

## Scene 1 (0:00 to 0:12)

Three shopkeepers I spoke to sell on credit, and they keep it all in a notebook. Every month someone says they already paid. Each of them has lost a customer over it.

## Scene 2 (0:12 to 0:40)

This is Fiado. In this practice shop, Bisi has a limit of ten thousand naira and already owes three thousand two hundred.

I'll try to put eight thousand eight hundred more on the tab.

*(pause while the stamp lands)*

The network refuses it. Not the shop, and not me. Stellar itself says this payment goes over the limit, and nothing is added. Here is the refused transaction, on the public ledger.

## Scene 3 (0:40 to 1:05)

The credit limit is a trustline limit on Stellar. When a customer buys on credit, they sign the payment themselves. So only the customer can write a debt. The shop can't make one up, and the customer can't say they never agreed. The shop pays the fees, so the customer needs no money and no app. Every claim on this page has a link, so you can check it yourself.

## Scene 4 (1:05 to 1:35)

Now a normal day. A customer buys five hundred naira of goods on credit and signs. The amount owed goes up. Later, they bring cash. I mark it paid, and the amount goes back down.

That is the whole book: what was bought, what was paid, and who agreed to it.

*Optional, if you use the two-phone clip:* The shop shows a code. The customer scans it, signs, and appears in my book with a limit I chose.

## Scene 5 (1:35 to 1:55)

The record belongs to the customer, not to one shop's notebook. It lives on the ledger, so a new shop can read what this customer bought, what they repaid, and how long it took. There is no score and no middleman.

## Scene 6 (1:55 to 2:15)

Three shopkeepers in Lagos have agreed to be listed here: Amina, Emeka and Chinedu. Their notebook figures are shown on this page, marked clearly as paper records and not on the ledger yet. Everything above them is counted from the network.

This runs on testnet, so no real money moves. It is live now at fiado-delta dot vercel dot app.

## Things to keep true when you speak

- Say "practice shop" in scene 2. It is a real transaction on testnet, but the shop and the customers are made up for the demo, and the screen says Demo.
- Say "testnet" once, clearly, in scene 6. Do not say mainnet, interest, credit scores or USDC.
- Do not say the passkeys were tested on real phones.
- Do not read a customer's real name. Bisi is a practice customer.
